## Most influential parameters (over the hypothetical ranges)
- **Net proliferation r and immune killing β dominate**: restricted event time S_T(r) = 0.78, S_T(β) = 0.33; event indicator 0.78 / 0.43 (Sobol N = 2048). Next: q (S_T 0.12 / 0.20), ρ (0.08 / 0.16), detection threshold (0.08 / 0.11), initial burden N₀ (0.06 / 0.10; dominant for log N(240) after r and β: S_T 0.17).
- **a, δ_D, η and the initial dormant fraction f_D are close to non-influential** (S_T ≤ 0.05).
- **Strong interactions** (S_T − S₁ ≈ 0.25 for r, 0.21 for β for restricted time; 0.36 and 0.30 for the event), but only one second-order pair is resolved (r×β ≈ 0.10, event indicator); for burden and timing the interaction variance is spread over many pairs/higher orders. Spearman, PRCC, Morris and Sobol agree on the top three.
- **Locally, at the baseline reference** (β = 0.04, first crossing 219.9 months): elasticity of N(240) r +9.9, β −4.1, q −2.5, ρ −1.1; **a 3.3% decrease of r removes the event by 240 months** (event time derivative with respect to r undefined). Initial burden composition matters locally (D₀ ≈ 10× P₀, because the start is 99% dormant) but not globally (f_D negligible; total N₀ matters).
- Under the **P1 prior** (r fixed), β (S_T 0.63), q (0.34), ρ (0.18), N₀ (0.14) drive event-time uncertainty. The ranking depends on which inputs are given distributions.

## Bistability and basin findings
- Both kinds of Phase 2 bistability were mapped; all attractor labels reproduce under tighter numerics (100% agreement, 0 unresolved, no flips under 10⁻⁶ jitter).
- **A locally stable tumour-free state does not guarantee control from every initial condition**, but in the baseline-adjacent case (η = 0.6) the nonzero basin starts only at N₀ ≳ 0.15 (saddle 0.1495): all below-threshold starts at θ = 0.1 end at tumour-free; at θ = 0.5, 290 of 389 below-threshold starts in the NZ basin relapse.
- **Two nonzero states:** baseline-adjacent (η = 0.164) high basin needs N₀ ≳ 0.2. With strong stimulation (ρ/μ = 4.8) **8.5% of below-threshold random starts reach the high attractor and all relapse**; immune depletion (e₀ = 0.3) lowers the boundary from N₀ = 0.071 to 0.039.
- **Event ≠ attractor**: transient exceedance occurs from the low basin (B2: 8 of 1,895 at θ = 0.1; 262 of 1,325 at θ = 0.01; B1 19 of 2,071), and the outcome of the relapse definition depends on where the threshold lies relative to the saddle.
- Bistable regimes are rare under P1 (1.8% of samples; NZ+NZ 1.6%, TF+NZ 0.2%).

## Detection-threshold sensitivity (θ = 0.01 … 0.5)
(run-A samples, n = 5000; 'P(60) = 0' below means < about 1/5000.) P(240) falls from **0.689 to 0.214**; P(120) from 0.352 to 0.008; P(60) from 0.040 to 0; conditional median crossing time rises 118.6 → 185.6 months. Transient crossings are ≤ 1.5% of events at every threshold and do not change conclusions. The attractor-based predictor matches long-horizon events at 99.4–100% at every threshold; the k_c-type rule ("tumour-free unstable") has accuracy 0.84 against long-horizon events (specificity 0.20; 804 of 5000 samples are unstable but never reach θ = 0.1) and falls to 0.47 against 240-month events. **The detection boundary is sample-specific**: per-sample β_det/β_c at θ = 0.1 has median 0.72, IQR 0.58–0.86, range 0.07–1.04; the Phase 2 baseline k_det/k_c = 0.7548 is verified at baseline for all seven thresholds only, and must not be applied to other parameter vectors.

## Convergence and uncertainty
- Monte Carlo running estimates stabilise by n ≈ 2000; five independent 1000-batches give P(240) 0.423–0.460 (SE 0.016); single 5000-runs of the same prior give 0.424, 0.442, 0.443 (homogeneity p = 0.10), so seeds matter at the second decimal and the pooled n = 15,000 value (0.436 ± 0.008) is the headline. The 5-year probability needs pooling: 0, 4, 4 crossings in three 5000-runs.
- Global ranking order of the top inputs is stable from n ≈ 2000 (Kendall τ 0.82; bootstrap half-width 0.123 → 0.028 for n = 250 → 4000); Sobol S_T(β) stays within 0.27–0.33 for N ≥ 256 (S₁ converges more slowly).
- Solver tolerances (rtol 10⁻⁶…10⁻¹², Radau, LSODA; 600 samples): **0 event flips**, max |ΔT| = 0.0067 months; reproducible (same seed identical; parallel = serial); 0 numerical failures in all runs.
- **Separated sources:** MC sampling error (± 0.014 at 5000), prior-assumption spread (0.15), threshold assumption (0.47), numerical (≤ 0.007 months). Assumptions, not sampling, dominate.

## Limitations and unresolved issues
- Ranges and priors are hypothetical; sensitivity rankings inherit them (r: ×10, β: ×20, N₀: ×5000). First-order Sobol intervals are wide; near-zero inputs are not rank-resolved.
- PRCC/Spearman assume monotone responses (checked graphically for the top inputs only).
- Basins are 2-D slices of the 3-D initial-condition space for three parameter sets; slice fractions are not probabilities. Bistable regimes are rare under P1, so their event probabilities rest on n = 90 samples.
- Deterministic model: Monte Carlo spread = parameter and initial-condition uncertainty, not demographic stochasticity; no treatment, constant parameters, binary dormancy, mass-action killing.
- The long-run classifier clips the RHS to the orthant and assumes convergence to an equilibrium.
- Detection uses a 0.05-month grid; the per-sample boundary scan uses 300 samples and a 5% β grid.
- Dormancy duration is an operational, hypothetical descriptor.
- Phase 3 did not examine model-structure alternatives (M2–M8), stochastic extinction, or the METABRIC comparison.

## Phase 4 verdict and recommendations
**Verdict: Phase 4 can begin as identifiability and model-structure work (synthetic recovery, endpoint/time-origin mapping, nuisance priors on the detection threshold and initial burden); it should not begin as calibration of these simulated probabilities to patient data.**
1. Clinical calibration is deferred: the probabilities above are prior-dependent (assumption spread 0.15 at 20 years) and threshold-dependent (0.69 → 0.21).
2. Decide the event definition and time origin for the clinical comparison first (endpoint = recurrence-free survival with unknown treatment offset τ, M8), and define a simulated-to-observed mapping before any fitting; the threshold alone shifts P(20 y) by a factor 3.
3. Synthetic recovery / profile-likelihood tests: which of (r, β, q, ρ, N₀) are identifiable from a time-to-event curve with the threshold unknown; use the normalised β and N₀ as sampling coordinates. The global results suggest only a few combinations (r, β, q, threshold) matter, with strong interactions.
4. Treat the threshold, the initial burden distribution and the choice of fixed parameters (r, μ, h) as explicit nuisance assumptions with their own priors; report the prior-assumption spread next to every probability.
5. Extend the Monte Carlo with stochastic birth–death–switching (M6) for small populations before calibrating, since all present variability is parameter variability only.
6. Keep bistability in scope but stress it deliberately (priors with ρ/μ and η that produce NZ+NZ and TF+NZ regimes in ≥ 10–20% of draws), because P1 hardly samples them.
7. Retain the dense-runner/`simulate` cross-check and the config-hash provenance in all later phases.
