## 13. Clinical Dataset Investigation

This section documents what was established about the one clinical endpoint dataset that is candidate for a comparison with the model, and what could not be established. **Nothing in this section is clinical validation of the model.** No model parameter was fitted to, or tuned using, any clinical record. The patient-level analyses reported here are a descriptive audit of a public export (Section 13.1–13.7); the remaining subsections (13.8–13.12) are *synthetic* studies in which the ground truth is known, designed to ask whether a comparison with such an endpoint could ever be informative. Each statement is tagged as **verified** (computed or read from a source), **inference**, or **unknown**. Simulated probabilities in Sections 10 and 13.9–13.11 are properties of a hypothetical parameter prior; they are not patient risks.

### 13.1 Dataset and audit procedure

Two public clinical exports from the cBioPortal data hub were downloaded on 2026-11-03 and audited by `experiments/metabric_audit.py` and `experiments/tcga_audit.py` (raw files in `data/raw/` are unmodified; no patient-level processed table was created). The METABRIC breast-cancer cohort [@Curtis2012; @Pereira2016; @Rueda2019; @METABRICcBio] contains 2,509 patients, of whom 2,380 have both an RFS time and an RFS status and are **usable** (129 are excluded: 13 miss both fields, 108 miss the time, 8 miss the status). The TCGA-BRCA PanCancer Atlas export [@TCGAcBio] contains 1,084 patients and was examined as an alternative.

**TCGA-BRCA outcome (verified).** The TCGA export documents its disease-free status as "since initial treatment", a better-defined origin than METABRIC's. It has 941 usable disease-free-survival records with only 84 events, median observed time 25.0 months, and only 190, 29 and 4 patients followed for at least 60, 120 and 240 months; 10 disease-free-survival events occur after 60 months and 1 after 120 months. It is therefore unsuitable for long-latency relapse and was not used for any comparison. The two datasets were **not combined** because their endpoints and time origins are not reconcilable. The curated TCGA clinical data resource and the original METABRIC data from the European Genome-phenome Archive were not examined.

The Phase 1 audit gave a conditional GO for a group-level, exploratory comparison at 5 and 10 years (15 and 20 years with caution) and a NO-GO for patient-level prediction and for any claim about recovering dormant-cell parameters (`data/dataset_feasibility.md`, Section 8).

### 13.2 The relapse-free-survival endpoint

**Verified (export metadata, verbatim).** `RFS_STATUS`: "Relapse Free Status (Recurred = cases where there was loco-regional relapse, distant relapse or death-specific death)"; `RFS_MONTHS`: "Time until last follow-up or relapse (in months)"; `OS_MONTHS`: "Overall survival in months since initial diagnosis". Consequently:

* The event is a **composite**: loco-regional relapse, distant relapse, or disease-specific death. Deaths from other causes are censored. Recurrence and death are not separately timed, and loco-regional versus distant relapse cannot be distinguished.
* The origin of overall survival is initial diagnosis; **the origin of RFS is not stated.**
* The model's event is different: the first upward crossing of a burden threshold $N=N_{\det}$ by a post-treatment residual population (Section 8). The two differ in event definition (composite, competing deaths), in time origin (post-treatment $t=0$ versus an unknown clinical origin) and in detection (a threshold on an unobserved burden versus a clinical diagnosis). In this report the clinical target is always called "RFS as defined in the cBioPortal METABRIC export", never "recurrence", "disease-free survival" or "overall survival".

### 13.5 Time-origin uncertainty: verified facts and inferences

A first temptation is to read the observation that RFS is never longer than OS as evidence that both are measured from diagnosis. This inference is **not** drawn here, because it is also satisfied by many other origins shared by the two clocks (for example surgery) and by a clock that merely starts earlier or later than diagnosis for both. A more specific, falsifiable test was used instead: if two clocks share an origin and both end at the same last-contact date, then RFS and OS must be exactly equal for patients with no recorded relapse who are alive or die of other causes. Table 13.3 reports the result.

**Table 13.3.** Common-origin test of RFS against OS (source: results/tables/p4_metabric_origin_tests.csv).

| Group | $n$ with both times | RFS = OS | RFS < OS | RFS > OS (violations) | Median OS − RFS when RFS < OS (months) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Not recurred & living | 745 | 745 | 0 | 0 | – |
| Not recurred & died of other causes | 432 | 432 | 0 | 0 | – |
| Recurred & died of disease | 646 | 101 | 545 | 0 | 15.5 |
| Recurred & living | 92 | 3 | 89 | 0 | 98.2 |
| Recurred & died of other causes | 65 | 0 | 65 | 0 | 49.4 |
| All with both times | 1,980 | 1,281 | 699 | 0 | 20.6 |

Equality holds for 745 of 745 and 432 of 432 patients, with zero violations across all 1,980 patients with both times. The medians in the last column are conditional on death or recurrence, are therefore biased and are not delay distributions.

**Verified:** OS origin is diagnosis; the composite event definition; the absence of treatment dates and of a loco-regional versus distant split; exact equality of RFS and OS in the two groups above. **Inference (not proof):** RFS and OS share one clock. Equality is consistent with diagnosis *or* surgery (separated by days to weeks, unrecorded) as the common origin; it excludes neither and it does not suggest the end of treatment. **Unknown:** the RFS origin, the diagnosis-to-treatment interval, the recurrence-to-detection delay, and the death-first share beyond the bounds of Section 13.6. A time-boxed search of the export metadata, the open full texts of the three METABRIC publications, and the data hub commit history found no statement of the RFS origin (the data hub history records "Added RFS data" and "Update RFS STATUS description" with no source; the original supplementary methods were not accessible). One of those publications [@Rueda2019] defines a multistate model whose disease-free state starts at surgery but says nothing about the export's RFS field.

*Source discrepancy (noted, not resolved).* Phase 1 reported "all 1,981" patients with RFS ≤ OS and 1,282 with equality; the Phase 4 recomputation used 1,980 patients with both times and found 1,281 equal. The one-patient difference is not explained in the stored outputs; both give zero violations, and neither affects any conclusion. Table 13.3 uses the Phase 4 count.

### 13.7 Treatment information

Chemotherapy, hormone therapy, radiotherapy and breast surgery are present only as binary or categorical flags (1,980, 1,980, 1,980 and 1,955 non-missing records, respectively), with **no dates and no durations** (source: p4_metabric_treatment_fields.csv). The interval between diagnosis and the start of the model's post-treatment state cannot be reconstructed from the export and is carried as a nuisance parameter $\tau$ below. These flags are outcome-correlated clinical variables and were never used as predictors.

### 13.8 From model event to clinical endpoint: an explicit mapping

The observation hierarchy used in Phase 4 separates five levels: hidden states $(P,D,e)$; the burden $N=P+D$; noisy, left-censored measurement signals; the threshold-defined event; and the clinical outcome. For the last step the hypothetical mapping is

$$
t_{\mathrm{clin}} = \tau + t_{\mathrm{model}} + d \quad\text{(recurrence)},\qquad t_{\mathrm{clin}} = \tau + t_{\mathrm{model}} + L \quad\text{(disease-specific death first)},
\tag{13.1}
$$

where $\tau$ is an unknown offset between the clinical origin and the model origin, $d$ a detection delay and $L$ a lag to death. These are **nuisance assumptions, not estimates**: no distribution for $\tau$, $d$ or $L$ was inferred, because the data to do so do not exist. Because $\tau$ and $d$ enter only through $\tau+d$, the clinical cumulative event curve is the model curve translated by $\tau+d$ — an exact algebraic fact, shown numerically below.

**Figure 13.1.** Endpoint mapping: documented quantities versus nuisances, with the descriptive METABRIC curve. *Setting:* schematic (left) and the overall Kaplan–Meier estimate of RFS in the export, $n=2{,}380$, 964 events, no model fitted or overlaid (right). *Axes:* left, a schematic ordering of events in time (not to scale), with double-headed arrows marking the unknown windows $\tau$, $d$ and $L$; right, months from the undocumented RFS origin against Kaplan–Meier cumulative event probability, with 95% intervals and at-risk counts at 60, 120, 180 and 240 months. *Shows:* the right panel reproduces Table 13.1 (cumulative event probabilities 0.263, 0.376, 0.456, 0.505 with 1,581, 984, 521 and 162 at risk). ### 13.9 Synthetic survival validation

Before any clinical comparison could be informative, it must be shown that the survival estimators recover a known event-time distribution under the censoring patterns of a cohort like the clinical one. The truth was the pooled hypothetical prior-P1 population of Phase 3 (three independent runs, 15,000 simulated individuals; cumulative first-crossing probabilities $F(60)=0.0005$, $F(120)=0.0910$, $F(240)=0.4363$). In total 200 cohorts of $n=2{,}380$ (and $n=1{,}000$) were resampled under administrative censoring, random loss to follow-up, and interval censoring from annual or six-monthly visits.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kaplan–Meier, administrative at 240 mo | −0.0002 | +0.0007 | 0.970 | 0.960 |
| Kaplan–Meier, administrative 240 mo plus random loss | −0.0003 | +0.0005 | 0.945 | 0.965 |
| Turnbull NPMLE, annual visits | −0.0002 | +0.0007 | not computed | not computed |
| Turnbull NPMLE, 6-monthly visits | −0.0002 | +0.0007 | not computed | not computed |
| Naive (censored treated as event-free), random loss | −0.0171 | −0.1158 | – | – |

Monte Carlo standard errors of the bias are 0.0004–0.0008. The 60-month results are degenerate (the truth is 0.0005, about one event per cohort) and are not interpreted. Turnbull interval coverage at $n=2{,}380$ was **not computed** (a bootstrap was run only for $n=1{,}000$, 50 cohorts, giving coverage 0.90 at 120 and 240 months). ### 13.13 Limitations of the clinical investigation

1. The RFS origin, diagnosis-to-treatment interval, detection delay and death-first share are unknown; the nuisance values used are not estimates. Records with missing vital status (161 events) limit the death-versus-recurrence bounds.
2. The composite endpoint, competing-risk censoring and cohort heterogeneity differ from the model's single threshold-crossing event; no competing-risk (Aalen–Johansen) analysis was performed here (Section 19 reports the restricted descriptive analysis that follows).
3. Subgroup curves are interpretable only where Table 13.2 says so; 21% of patients lack extended fields.
4. The synthetic validations used one hypothetical prior, a single death-lag assumption, correct model structure, known censoring mechanisms and finite replicate numbers; Turnbull coverage at $n=2{,}380$ and informative censoring were not studied.
5. The Kaplan–Meier estimator was hand-written; its correctness is supported by the recovery of the synthetic truth (Table 13.5) and a worked example in the test suite, not by comparison with an external library.
6. None of these results is clinical validation, and none gives a patient-level risk or a recurrence prediction.
