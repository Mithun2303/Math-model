## 7. Bifurcation Analysis

This section asks how the long-time behaviour of the P–D–e model changes as the immune-killing strength $k$ (equivalently the normalised killing rate $\beta=ks/\mu$) and the immune-suppression rate $\eta$ vary. It rests on three ingredients: the exact equilibrium structure of Section 5, the Jacobian analysis of Section 6, and a numerical continuation code whose output is checked against the closed-form branch. Throughout, the parameter values are the hypothetical baseline set ($r=0.12$, $q=0.04$, $a=0.005$, $\delta_D=0.001$, $k=0.1$, $s=0.02$, $\mu=0.05$, $\rho=0.04$, $h=0.02$, $\eta=0.08$, $K=1$; rates in month$^{-1}$) unless a different value is stated. No number in this section has an empirical basis; the *structure* of the results is what is intended to transfer.

### 7.1 Equilibrium branches used in the analysis

Two constants recur. With $c=q/(a+\delta_D)$, $\varphi=1+c$ and $r_\mathrm{eff}=r-q\delta_D/(a+\delta_D)$, every nonzero equilibrium satisfies $D^*=cP^*$, $N^*=\varphi P^*$, $E^*=s/B(P^*)$ and lies on the explicit graph (derived in Section 5)

$$
k=k(P)=\frac{A(P)\,B(P)}{s},\qquad A(P)=r_\mathrm{eff}-\frac{r\varphi}{K}P,\qquad B(P)=\mu+\eta\varphi P-\frac{\rho P}{h+P}.\tag{7.1}
$$

Because $k$ enters linearly, (7.1) gives *all* nonzero equilibria for *all* $k$ at once, and the equilibrium condition is a cubic in $P$, so there are at most three nonzero equilibria (a proved bound). The tumour-free equilibrium is $(0,0,E_f)$ with $E_f=s/\mu$. At baseline $c=6.667$, $\varphi=7.667$, $r_\mathrm{eff}=0.11333$ and $E_f=0.4$.

**Numerical checks of the branch structure** (a numerical, not a symbolic, statement). A pseudo-arclength continuation code (adaptive step, Newton corrector judged on the Newton step rather than the residual, bisection location of sign changes of $\det J$, of the Hopf function $a_1a_2-a_3$ and of $P$) traced the baseline branch with 217 points, maximum residual $|F|=8.7\times10^{-18}$ and maximum deviation from (7.1) of $2.5\times10^{-16}$. Reducing the step cap from 0.2 to 0.01 left the branch accuracy unchanged and kept the branch-point error at or below $7\times10^{-10}$ (Table 7.1). An independent search that knew nothing of the cubic (300 random log-uniform starts per value of $k$) found the same number of equilibria at all eight tested ratios $k/k_c$, with residuals at most $6\times10^{-18}$ (e.g. one nonzero equilibrium for $k/k_c\le0.999$ and none for $k/k_c=1.001$ and $1.5$).

### 7.2 The transcritical bifurcation at $k=k_c$

#### 7.2.1 Definition

For $\dot x=F(x,k)$ and an equilibrium branch $x_0(k)$, a **transcritical bifurcation** at $(x_0,k_0)$ requires (Sotomayor's conditions for a branch that exists on both sides of $k_0$): a simple zero eigenvalue of $J=F_x(x_0,k_0)$ with right and left null vectors $w,\ell$ ($\ell\cdot w=1$) and all other eigenvalues off the imaginary axis, together with

1. $\ell\cdot F_k(x_0,k_0)=0$ (the equilibrium persists through $k_0$);
2. *transversality*: $\alpha=\ell\cdot\big[F_{xk}\,w\big]\neq0$;
3. *nondegeneracy*: $\beta_2=\tfrac12\,\ell\cdot D^2F(w,w)\neq0$.

On the one-dimensional centre manifold the dynamics reduce to $\dot u=\alpha(k-k_0)u+\beta_2u^2+\cdots$. The branches $u=0$ and $u^*=-\alpha(k-k_0)/\beta_2$ intersect at $k_0$ and exchange stability, since the two eigenvalues are $\alpha(k-k_0)$ and $-\alpha(k-k_0)$. (The symbol $\beta_2$ is used here for the quadratic coefficient to avoid confusion with the effective killing rate $\beta=ks/\mu$.)

#### 7.2.2 Derivation for the model

At $x_0=(0,0,E_f)$ the $E$-column of the Jacobian vanishes in the $P$ and $D$ rows (the only entries there, $-kP$ and $0$, are zero at $P=0$), so the $(P,D)$ block decouples:

$$
J=\begin{pmatrix} r-q-kE_f & a & 0\\ q & -(a+\delta_D) & 0\\ (\rho/h-\eta)E_f & -\eta E_f & -\mu\end{pmatrix},\qquad \operatorname{spec}J=\{-\mu\}\cup\operatorname{spec}M,\tag{7.2}
$$

with $M=\begin{pmatrix} r-q-\beta & a\\ q&-(a+\delta_D)\end{pmatrix}$ and $\beta=kE_f$ (this is the same normalised killing rate $ks/\mu$ introduced in Section 4). Then $\det M=-(a+\delta_D)(r-q-\beta)-aq$ vanishes exactly when $\beta=r_\mathrm{eff}$, i.e.

$$
k_c=\frac{r_\mathrm{eff}\,\mu}{s}=0.28333\ldots\tag{7.3}
$$

and $\operatorname{tr}M<0$ there, so the remaining eigenvalues are $-\mu=-0.05$ and $-0.03933$: the zero eigenvalue is simple and the others lie in the open left half-plane. The null vectors are $w=(1,c,w_E)$ with $w_E=E_f[\rho/h-\eta\varphi]/\mu=11.093$, and $\ell=(\ell_P,\ell_D,0)$ with $\ell_P=0.15254$, $\ell_D=0.12712$. All derivative quantities were evaluated with the analytic Jacobian, analytic $F_k=(-EP,0,0)$ and analytic second derivative $D^2F$, the latter cross-checked against symbolic differentiation in the automated tests.

$$

$$

The centre-manifold slope and the exact slope agree to the printed 10 digits (and to 16 digits in the underlying computation). Hence the Sotomayor conditions hold at baseline, and the point is a transcritical bifurcation of the system extended to $P\in(-h,\infty)$.

*Numerical confirmation.* At twelve values $k=k_c(1\pm\varepsilon)$, $\varepsilon\in[10^{-4},10^{-1}]$, exactly one of the two branches is stable for every sampled $k$. For $k<k_c$ the tumour-free state is a saddle and the nonzero state is stable; for $k>k_c$ the tumour-free state is stable and the algebraic continuation of the nonzero root has $P<0$ and is unstable. The ratios of the computed critical eigenvalues to the predicted $\pm\alpha(k-k_c)$ approach 1 (1.0002 and 0.9997 at $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$), and the relative error of the linear prediction $P^*\approx(k-k_c)/k'(0)$ falls from 17.9% at $\varepsilon=0.1$ to $1.7\times10^{-4}$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$, i.e. first-order convergence as expected for a truncated expansion (Table 7.3).

#### 7.2.3 What may and may not be claimed

* *Established:* a simple zero eigenvalue at $k_c$; intersecting branches; transversal eigenvalue crossing ($\alpha\neq0$); $\beta_2\neq0$; exchange of stability. These are verified analytically, by continuation and by root enumeration.
* *Qualification:* on the nonzero branch $D^*=cP^*$, so the branch passes through the origin of the $(P,D)$ plane and $P$ changes sign at $k_c$ whenever $k'(0)\ne0$. A transcritical exchange between two *admissible* branches on both sides is therefore impossible. In the biological orthant $P,D,E\ge0$ the picture at baseline is a **forward (supercritical) branching of a stable low-burden state out of the tumour-free state**: below $k_c$ a stable low-burden state exists, above $k_c$ only the tumour-free state is admissible. The label "transcritical" is the correct local normal form; its biological reading is "branching off at $k_c$".
* *Direction is not universal* (Section 7.3): for $k'(0)>0$ the same point is still transcritical but backward (subcritical), and the nonzero branch lives on $k>k_c$.

### 7.3 Saddle-node bifurcations and the two kinds of bistability

#### 7.3.1 Method

Because (7.1) is a graph over $P$, a complete scan over $k$ is possible by sampling $P$ over the admissible range ($A>0$, $B>0$) and evaluating, at each $P$, (i) $dk/dP$ (sign changes mark saddle-nodes; the zeros are roots of an explicit quadratic–cubic expression), (ii) Routh–Hurwitz stability of the characteristic polynomial $\lambda^3+a_1\lambda^2+a_2\lambda+a_3$, and (iii) the Hopf function $H=a_1a_2-a_3$ (a sign change with $a_2>0$ indicates a purely imaginary pair). Two Latin-hypercube scans of 8,000 log-uniform draws each were run: scan A varies $(a,q,\rho,\eta)$; scan B additionally varies $(r,\delta_D,\mu,h)$ (ranges in Table 7.4). Draws with $r_\mathrm{eff}\le0$ are excluded as invalid.

#### 7.3.2 Backward bifurcation

*Proved* from (7.4): $k'(0)>0$ (backward transcritical bifurcation) if and only if

$$
\eta>\eta^*=\frac{1}{\varphi}\Big[\frac{\rho}{h}+\frac{r\varphi\mu}{K\,r_\mathrm{eff}}\Big]=0.3138,\qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad \rho<\rho^*=h\Big(\eta\varphi-\frac{r\varphi\mu}{K\,r_\mathrm{eff}}\Big)=0.00415,\tag{7.5}
$$

all other parameters at baseline. At $\eta=\eta^*$ one has $\beta_2=0$: the transcritical point is degenerate (a codimension-two point), and (7.5) defines the black line in Figure 7.3 (middle panel). In the scans, backward behaviour at $k_c$ occurs in 1,676 of 7,278 (scan A) and 1,395 of 6,777 (scan B) valid draws (Table 7.4).

For $\eta=0.6$ the continuation locates a fold at $k_\mathrm{SN}=0.860834$ ($P=0.05849$, $D=0.38991$, $N=0.4484$), matching the exact cubic root to $10^{-8}$. Between $k_c=0.2833$ and $k_\mathrm{SN}$ the stable tumour-free state coexists with a stable high-burden state separated by an unstable branch. Simulations confirm this (Table 7.5): from small residual disease ($P_0=10^{-6}$, $D_0=10^{-4}$, $E_0=E_f$) the system goes to the tumour-free state, while from a large initial burden $(0.3,0.3,E=0.1)$ it goes to the high-burden state; at $k=0.5k_c$, where only one stable state exists, both initial conditions reach the same nonzero state.

#### 7.3.3 Fold pairs with a forward transcritical point: two distinct kinds of coexistence

For smaller $\eta$ (or larger $\rho$) the nonzero branch can be S-shaped without being backward at $k_c$ ($k'(0)<0$). Where the two folds lie relative to $k_c$ decides what coexists (verified by exact root enumeration, eigenvalue labelling and simulation):

* *Tumour-free + nonzero bistability* (kind 1): requires a stable nonzero state at some $k>k_c$, e.g. $\eta=0.6$ above.
* *Two stable nonzero states, tumour-free unstable* (kind 2): requires a fold pair lying below $k_c$. At $\eta=0.12$ the folds are at $k=0.22933$ and $0.23025$, both below $k_c$; in this narrow interval there are three nonzero equilibria (stable, saddle, stable) while the tumour-free state is a saddle.
* At $\eta=0.164$ the folds are at $k=0.2561$ (below $k_c$) and $0.2840$ (above $k_c$). On $(0.2561,0.2833)$ two stable nonzero states (a focus at $P=0.0026$ and a node at $P=0.065$, at $k=0.27$) coexist with a saddle ($P=0.027$) and an *unstable* tumour-free state; only on $(0.2833,0.2840)$ do a stable tumour-free state and one stable nonzero state coexist (e.g. saddle $P=0.0447$ and stable node $P=0.0506$ at $k=0.28367$).
* The first fold appears at $\eta\approx0.116$; tumour-free + nonzero bistability onsets at $\eta\approx0.164$ (or $\rho\lesssim0.005$ at baseline $\eta$). The baseline ($\eta=0.08,\rho=0.04$) has neither kind of coexistence, roughly a factor of 2 below the onset in $\eta$.

#### 7.3.4 Two stable nonzero equilibria at one $k$

Multistability among nonzero states occurs in $1{,}349+1{,}206=2{,}555$ of $7{,}278+6{,}777=14{,}055$ valid draws ($\approx18\%$), and in *every* such case the coexistence window lies below $k_c$, where the tumour-free state is a saddle. An example from scan A is $a=0.306$, $q=1.6\times10^{-3}$, $\rho=0.240$, $\eta=1.637$ at $k=0.15$: three nonzero roots $P=0.0027$ (stable focus), $0.111$ (saddle) and $0.977$ (stable node) plus an unstable tumour-free state; 30 random initial conditions integrated for $3\times10^5$ months ended only at $P=0.0027$ or $0.977$. The mechanism is strong immune stimulation ($\rho/\mu=4.8$ here): a low-burden immune-controlled state versus a near-carrying-capacity state. A larger sample of example draws is in results/tables/parameter_scan_examples.csv.

### 7.4 Stability regions and the dormant fraction

Synthesising Sections 7.2–7.3, for the baseline parameters and $0<k<k_c$ the unique nonzero equilibrium is stable and the tumour-free state is a saddle; for $k>k_c$ the tumour-free state is the only admissible equilibrium and is stable. For $\eta>\eta^*$ (or $\rho<\rho^*$) the tumour-free state is stable for all $k>k_c$ and a second stable branch exists up to the fold. For parameter sets with a fold pair below $k_c$, two stable nonzero equilibria coexist on a window below $k_c$. The three regimes occupy distinct regions of the $(\eta,\rho)$ plane (Figure 7.3). All stability labels were obtained from the full set of Jacobian eigenvalues (not only the largest real part).

A further exact structural result concerns the proportion of dormant cells: on every nonzero equilibrium $D^*/N^*=c/(1+c)=q/(q+a+\delta_D)=0.8696$ for every $k$. This is an *identity* of the switching rates, not an outcome of immune control (Figure 7.4, left). By contrast the dormant fraction at the moment of detection along transient trajectories (right panel) varies strongly with $k$.

### 7.5 Oscillations and the Hopf bifurcation: search and limitations

* *Along the exact branch graph* in both scans: no sign change of $a_1a_2-a_3$ with $a_2>0$ in any of 14,055 valid parameter sets (0 Hopf candidates). Stable *foci* (complex eigenvalues with negative real part, i.e. damped oscillation) occurred in 354 sets examined for this (198 in scan A and 156 in scan B, from a subsample of draws), but none crosses the imaginary axis.
* *Events recorded as "neutral saddle"* ($a_1a_2=a_3$ with $a_2<0$) are crossings where two *real* eigenvalues sum to zero and are not Hopf bifurcations. At baseline they occur on the tumour-free branch at $k=0.0839$ and $k=0.1850$.
* *One Hopf candidate* was detected at $\eta=0.6$ ($k=0.2396$, $P=-0.0046$, $a_2>0$, eigenvalues $\pm0.0061\,i$), but on the *nonphysical* branch with $P<0$; it is not an admissible state and is not reported as a model bifurcation.
* *Direct simulation:* 263 random draws of (parameters, $k\in[0,2k_c]$, initial condition) were integrated to $1.5\times10^4$ months; 15 were flagged by a crude amplitude criterion at the first stage and re-integrated to $3\times10^6$ months, after which all converged to an equilibrium with residual $\le10^{-17}$. Persistent oscillations found: 0 (results/tables/oscillation_search_summary.csv).

**Conclusion and limits.** "No Hopf bifurcation and no sustained oscillation were found in the scanned ranges." This is *not* a proof of absence: ranges are bounded and sampled, the oscillation search is small (263 draws), and no global argument (Dulac criterion, Lyapunov function) excludes limit cycles. Global boundedness of trajectories is also not proved (for $\rho>\mu$ the immune equation is not obviously dissipative), although none of the sampled trajectories diverged. These caveats matter because published minimal dormancy models report awakening and oscillatory regimes under other assumptions [@YanezFeliu2026]; our result concerns this specific three-compartment structure only.

## 8. Relapse-Time Analysis

### 8.1 Detection threshold and event definition

Throughout, a **relapse event** is the *first upward crossing* of a total burden $N=P+D$ through a prescribed detection threshold $N_\mathrm{det}$ (a fraction of $K$), starting from a post-treatment residual state $x_0=(P_0,D_0,E_f)$ with $P_0=10^{-6}$, $D_0=10^{-4}$ (initial burden $N_0=1.01\times10^{-4}$, 99% dormant) and the tumour-free immune level $E_f=s/\mu$. The numerical event time $T$ is the first time $N(t)=N_\mathrm{det}$ from below, computed with DOP853 (rtol $10^{-9}$) over a $10^5$-month horizon; this numerical first-crossing time is the reference ("truth") against which all analytic approximations are judged, and it is the quantity used later in the Monte Carlo analysis (Section 10). Thresholds studied are $N_\mathrm{det}\in\{10^{-3},10^{-2},0.1\}$. The detection threshold is a modelling assumption; nothing in the model identifies it with a clinical detection limit.

For $k<k_c$ the tumour-free state is a saddle with one positive real eigenvalue $\lambda_+$ (baseline $\lambda_+=0.02904$ month$^{-1}$ at $k=0.5k_c$; the other two are $-0.01171$ and $-0.05$). Let $w$ be the right eigenvector for $\lambda_+$ with $w_P=1$, $\ell$ the left eigenvector with $\ell\cdot w=1$, $n_1=w_P+w_D$, and $u_0=\ell\cdot(x_0-x_\mathrm{TF})$.

### 8.2 Analytical approximations

Four closed-form or semi-closed-form estimators were derived:

* **A0 (original, Phase 1):** $T_0=\ln(N_\mathrm{det}/N_0)/\lambda_+$, which assumes the whole initial burden lies on the growing mode.
* **A1 (eigen-projected):** the linear solution is $x-x_\mathrm{TF}=\sum_i c_iv_ie^{\lambda_it}$; the growing mode contributes $n_1u_0e^{\lambda_+t}$ to $N$, so

$$
T_1=\frac{1}{\lambda_+}\ln\frac{N_\mathrm{det}}{n_1u_0}.\tag{8.1}
$$

A1b solves $N_\mathrm{lin}(t)=N_\mathrm{det}$ exactly with both retained modes.

* **A2 (second-order unstable manifold):** the one-dimensional unstable manifold is $x=x_\mathrm{TF}+uw+u^2h_2$ with $\dot u=\lambda_+u+\gamma u^2$, $\gamma=\tfrac12\ell\cdot D^2F(w,w)$, and $(2\lambda_+I-J)h_2=\tfrac12D^2F(w,w)-\gamma w$. With $N(u)=n_1u+n_2u^2$, $n_2=h_{2,P}+h_{2,D}$, the Bernoulli equation for $\dot u$ (substitute $v=1/u$) gives the closed form

$$
T_2=\frac{1}{\lambda_+}\ln\frac{1/u_0+\gamma/\lambda_+}{1/u_\mathrm{det}+\gamma/\lambda_+},\tag{8.2}
$$

with $u_\mathrm{det}$ the smallest positive root of $N(u)=N_\mathrm{det}$. If the bracket is non-positive (the variable $u$ saturates at $u^*=-\lambda_+/\gamma$ before reaching $u_\mathrm{det}$) the estimator returns $\infty$ ("no relapse predicted"). Here $\gamma=-1.35$ at baseline.
* **A3 (matched; semi-analytic, not a closed form):** $T_3=T_2(N_\mathrm{match})+\tau$ with $N_\mathrm{match}=10^{-3}\ll\lambda_+/|\gamma|$ and the *tail* $\tau$ obtained by numerically integrating from the second-order-manifold point to $N_\mathrm{det}$. The closed-form part depends on the initial condition (via $u_0$); $\tau$ depends only on the parameters. The decomposition $T=T_\mathrm{lin}(x_0)+\tau(\text{parameters})$ is the structural result; at $N_\mathrm{det}=0.1$ the agreement of A3 with the numerical time is largely numerics agreeing with numerics, because the numerical tail is 67–81% of $T$.

### 8.4 Why the original estimate fails: eigenmode projection, saturation, immune feedback

For $k=0.5k_c$, $N_\mathrm{det}=0.1$, initial dormant fraction 0.99, the numerical time is $T=375.5$ months and A0 gives $237.5$, a gap of $138.0$ months. Table 8.3 decomposes the gap by switching mechanisms off one at a time.

**Table 8.3.** Attribution of the relapse-time gap (months; source: results/tables/relapse_time_attribution.csv). $E_\mathrm{ev}/E_f$ = immune level at the event relative to the tumour-free level.

| variant | numerical $T$ | A0 | A1 | A2 | $E_\mathrm{ev}/E_f$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 375.5 | 237.5 | 281.6 | $\infty$ | 1.40 |
| $\rho=\eta=0$ (no immune feedback) | 293.0 | 237.5 | 281.6 | 293.4 | 1.00 |
| $\rho=0$ only | 287.2 | 237.5 | 281.6 | 287.2 | 0.90 |
| $\eta=0$ only | 459.9 | 237.5 | 281.6 | $\infty$ | 1.59 |

The three contributions (an order-dependent split, since the nonlinear terms do not add exactly) are:

* *Eigenmode projection* (A1 − A0): $+44.0$ months, because 99% of the initial burden is dormant and only part of it lies on the growing mode ($\ell=(0.860,0.123,0)$).
* *Logistic saturation and other no-feedback nonlinearity* (numerical at $\rho=\eta=0$ minus A1): $+11.5$ months.
* *Immune feedback during growth* (baseline numerical minus numerical at $\rho=\eta=0$): $+82.5$ months; $E$ rises to $1.40E_f$ at detection, because stimulation $\rho$ exceeds suppression $\eta$ along the path, which slows growth.

The robust statement is that the projection explains roughly a third of the gap and the immune response about 60%; saturation is minor. The largest omitted mechanism is therefore the second-order immune response. Removing $\eta$ alone lengthens $T$ to 459.9 months (the immune boost is uncancelled, $E$ reaches $1.59E_f$), while removing $\rho$ alone shortens it to 287.2 months.

Why A2 fails at $N_\mathrm{det}=0.1$: the quadratic closure of $\rho P/(h+P)$ requires $P_\mathrm{det}/h$ small, but $P_\mathrm{det}/h=N_\mathrm{det}/(\varphi h)=0.65$ at $N_\mathrm{det}=0.1$ (outside the Taylor radius), versus 0.065 at $N_\mathrm{det}=10^{-2}$, where A2 succeeds.

**Conditions of validity** (for the baseline parameter set; thresholds scale with $\lambda_+/|\gamma|$ and $\varphi h$):

| estimator | requires | fails when |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | $N_0$ on the growing mode; $N_\mathrm{det}\ll\lambda_+/|\gamma|$; no feedback | dormant-dominated starts; $k$ near $k_\mathrm{det}$ |
| A1/A1b | $N_\mathrm{det}\ll n_1\lambda_+/|\gamma|\approx0.046$ and $\ll\varphi h=0.153$; $k\le0.9k_\mathrm{det}$ | $N_\mathrm{det}\gtrsim0.02$ (errors $\ge13\%$) |
| A2 | $N_\mathrm{det}\lesssim0.3\min(n_1\lambda_+/|\gamma|,\varphi h)\approx0.014$ | $N_\mathrm{det}=0.1$ ($P_\mathrm{det}/h=0.65$): returns $\infty$ |
| A3 | $N_\mathrm{match}\ll\lambda_+/|\gamma|,\varphi h$; numerical tail | semi-analytic; $\infty$ beyond $k_\mathrm{det}$ |

### 8.5 The detection-dependent threshold boundary $k_\mathrm{det}/k_c$

For $k\in(k_\mathrm{det},k_c)$ the stable nonzero equilibrium has $N^*<N_\mathrm{det}$, so an untreated trajectory settles below the detection threshold. The boundary $k_\mathrm{det}$ solves $N^*(k)=N_\mathrm{det}$, i.e. $k_\mathrm{det}=k(P=N_\mathrm{det}/\varphi)$ from (7.1). It depends on $N_\mathrm{det}$ and is smaller than $k_c$ (proved from (7.1) as a deterministic function; the numerical values at baseline are $k_\mathrm{det}/k_c=0.9954,\ 0.9568,\ 0.7548$ for $N_\mathrm{det}=10^{-3},10^{-2},0.1$). Thus the relevant validity boundary of every relapse estimate is $k_\mathrm{det}$, not $k_c$.

*Is "no event beyond $k_\mathrm{det}$" exact?* It requires the approach to $N^*$ to be monotone from below. Because the nonzero equilibrium is a stable *focus* over much of the range (weak damping), overshoot above $N^*$ is conceivable. A fine scan ($k/k_\mathrm{det}\in\{0.98,0.99,0.995,0.999,1.0,1.001,1.005,1.01,1.02,1.03,1.04,1.06\}$ × three initial dormant fractions × three thresholds = 108 runs, horizon $10^5$ months) found:

| $N_\mathrm{det}$ | $k_\mathrm{det}/k_c$ | events at $k/k_\mathrm{det}\le0.999$ | events at $k/k_\mathrm{det}\ge1.001$ | max $N$ along trajectories at $k>1.001k_\mathrm{det}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.001 | 0.9954 | all 12 of 12 | 0 of 18 | 0.000117 |
| 0.01 | 0.9568 | all 12 of 12 | 0 of 18 | 0.008812 |

(Counts are over the 12 runs per threshold at $k/k_\mathrm{det}\le0.999$ — four values × three compositions — and the 18 runs per threshold at $k/k_\mathrm{det}>1.001$ — six values × three compositions; in total 36 runs with events and 54 without at $k>1.001k_\mathrm{det}$.) For $N_\mathrm{det}=0.1$ and initial dormant fraction 0.99 the event time diverges as $k\to k_\mathrm{det}^-$: 2,113, 2,560, 3,002 and 4,024 months at $k/k_\mathrm{det}=0.98,0.99,0.995,0.999$, and 16,305 months at $k=k_\mathrm{det}$; no event occurs at $1.001k_\mathrm{det}$ or beyond.

*Consequence.* At baseline and for these initial conditions ($N_0\le0.09$, $E_0=E_f$) the no-event boundary coincides with $k_\mathrm{det}$. For $N_\mathrm{det}=0.1$ and $k\in(0.755,1)k_c$, A0 and A1 return finite times although the simulation never crosses (the pink region in Figure 8.1, G1), A2 returns $\infty$, and A3 returns $\infty$ because its tail never reaches $N_\mathrm{det}$. The hypothesis that long transients near $k_c$ would cause delayed relapse therefore does not hold for $N_\mathrm{det}=0.1$ at $k>0.755k_c$ at baseline. This is verified only for baseline parameters and these initial states; for strongly focus-like equilibria, or initial states with depleted $E$, overshoot above $N^*$ could place the boundary above $k_\mathrm{det}$ and has not been excluded.

### 8.6 Summary of relapse-time results and limitations

*Established.* (i) The relapse time has the structure $T=T_\mathrm{lin}(x_0)+\tau(\text{parameters})$ and depends on the initial condition only through $\ln u_0$ (proved within the A3 construction; numerical agreement). (ii) The Phase 1 estimate A0 is composition-blind and systematically biased; the eigen-projected A1 removes the composition bias. (iii) The immune feedback during growth is the largest omitted mechanism at $N_\mathrm{det}=0.1$. (iv) The validity boundary is $k_\mathrm{det}$.

*Limitations.* The accuracy sweep used baseline structural parameters only; the "no event beyond $k_\mathrm{det}$" scan was likewise baseline-only; $N_0$ was held fixed at $1.01\times10^{-4}$; A3 is semi-analytic and its small error at large thresholds is not independent evidence of a closed form; A2/A3 validity thresholds are specific to this parameter set. These estimators are mathematical diagnostics, not clinical predictors; the model has no calibration to patient data.
